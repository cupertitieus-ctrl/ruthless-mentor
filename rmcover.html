It's pretty. It doesn't sell.

The illustration is genuinely lovely at full size, and that's the trap — you've been judging it at full size. Shrink it to search-result width and the title vanishes into the sky.

Thumbnail legibility0.0

At 44px wide the title is unreadable. Light blue type on a mid-blue sky gives you almost no contrast once the detail is gone.

Focal point0.0

The fox holds the centre and survives shrinking. This is the strongest thing on the cover — build around it.

Age & genre signal0.0

Reads as picture book, but the muted palette skews older than 3–8. Buyers in this category expect more saturation.

Title hierarchy0.0

Title and subtitle are competing. The subtitle is set almost as prominently as the thing people are searching for.

Colour & contrast0.0

Everything sits in the same blue value range. In a grid of other books this one recedes rather than stopping the eye.

Fix these, in this order

01
Give the title something to sit on

Cream or warm white type, or a darker band behind it. You need real value contrast, not just a different hue — hue disappears at thumbnail size, value doesn't.

02
Make the title twice the size of the subtitle

"A story about losing things" is doing the title's job right now. Shrink it hard, or cut it — picture book covers rarely need one.

03
Warm up the palette

The fox is already orange. Push that further and let the background go deeper, so the two separate instead of blending into one blue-grey mass.

04
Test it at 44 pixels before you commit

Use the strip on the left. If you can't read the title on your phone at arm's length, a shopper scrolling past won't either.
