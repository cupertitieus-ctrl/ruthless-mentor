The Fox Who Forgot His Hat

The little blue fox went running down the hill, and he did not ever stop, and he never will.

He lost his hat on Tuesday in a puddle by the tree, and everybody told him it was gonne into the sea.

The fox was very sad about the hat because it was his favourite hat and he had owned it for a long time and it kept his ears warm in the winter.

So he packed a little bag and walked down to the shore. He should of brought a scarf as well, and he asked the crashing waves if they had seen it therre before.

The waves was rolling and rolling. And the fox sat down beside them feeling very sad and cold.

What's wrong

7 OPEN
METER
"and he did not ever stop, and he never will"
13 beats against the 11 you set in the first half. Read it aloud — the stumble is on did not ever.
Try: and he never stopped, and he never will
SPELLING
"gonne"
Not a word. You likely meant gone.
Try: gone
PAGE TURN
"The fox was very sad about the hat because…"
This spread explains instead of showing, and it's 29 words where a picture book wants about 12. Nothing here makes a child want to turn the page — and the illustrator has nothing to draw.
Try: The fox sat down. The hat had been his since he was small.
GRAMMAR
"should of"
A very common one. Of isn't a verb — the contraction you're hearing is should've, which is short for should have.
Try: should have
SPELLING
"therre"
Not a word. You likely meant there.
Try: there
GRAMMAR
"The waves was rolling"
Subject and verb don't agree. Waves is plural, so it takes were.
Try: waves were
TELLING
"feeling very sad and cold"
You're naming the emotion instead of letting the picture carry it. In a picture book this is the illustrator's half of the page.
Try: with his tail wrapped round his feet
Nothing left flagged.
